---
title: Interpreting the Charts
description: How to read each of the four standard output charts, using a real HINDCOPPER.NS run.
---

A full `core/pipeline.py` run against one symbol produces four charts, generated
in this order. All four below are from the same real `HINDCOPPER.NS` run
referenced throughout this section (Jan–Jul 2026 window, 133 daily bars).

## 1. Candles + MA20/MA50 + Bollinger Bands

Generated by `core/stock_explorer.py::plot_candles`, this is the first
exploratory chart, drawn immediately after indicators are computed.

<img src="/charts/candles-ma-bbands.png" alt="Candles, MA20/MA50, and Bollinger Bands" width={2000} height={1089} style={{ width: '100%', height: 'auto' }} />

**How to read it:**
- **Blue line** is Close. **Orange** is MA20 (fast), **green** is MA50 (slow).
  MA20 above MA50 = `uptrend` regime for that bar; below = `downtrend` — this is
  exactly the `regime_from()` calculation that tags every pattern (see
  [Indicators & regime](/docs/statistical/indicators)).
- **Shaded band** is the ±2σ Bollinger channel around a 20-bar SMA. Price
  pinned near the upper edge in a downtrend, or the lower edge in an uptrend,
  is what triggers `BB_SellFade`/`BB_BuyDip`.
- **Band width** is a volatility gauge on its own — note the sharp widening
  around the late-January spike to ~760, then a slow contraction through
  February-March as volatility died down. Wide bands mean ATR-based position
  sizing (see [Signal compilation & execution](/docs/statistical/signals-execution))
  will size positions smaller for the same risk budget.
- The MA20/MA50 crossing back and forth (visible ~April and ~June here) is
  exactly why this symbol's regime flips between uptrend/downtrend/neutral
  multiple times across the window — each flip changes which patterns are
  even eligible to qualify/gate that day.

## 2. RSI(14)

Generated by `core/stock_explorer.py::plot_momentum` (first of its two charts).

<img src="/charts/rsi.png" alt="RSI(14) with 70/30 reference lines" width={1982} height={570} style={{ width: '100%', height: 'auto' }} />

**How to read it:**
- Remember this is **not** Wilder-smoothed RSI (see
  [Indicators & regime](/docs/statistical/indicators)) — it reacts a bit faster
  than the RSI you may be used to from other platforms.
- **Red dashed line (70)** and **green dashed line (30)** are visual reference
  levels only — they are *not* the actual pattern trigger levels. The real
  triggers are the 40/60 crossings (`RSI_40_Up`/`RSI_60_Down`), which sit well
  inside these 70/30 lines.
- In this run, RSI touched ~83 in late April/early May (a real overbought
  reading by the 70-line) and ~18-21 in mid-June and mid-February — but neither
  extreme itself fires a pattern; only the subsequent **crossing back through
  40 or 60** does. Watch for RSI crossing up through 40 after a dip (visible
  early April, ~30→60 climb) — that's an `RSI_40_Up` momentum-resumption
  signal, not a "buy the oversold bounce" signal.

## 3. MACD(12, 26, 9)

Generated by `core/stock_explorer.py::plot_momentum` (second chart).

<img src="/charts/macd.png" alt="MACD with signal line and histogram" width={2000} height={563} style={{ width: '100%', height: 'auto' }} />

**How to read it:**
- Remember this is the **SMA-based** MACD variant (see
  [Indicators & regime](/docs/statistical/indicators)) — smoother and laggier
  than the EMA-based MACD most platforms default to.
- **Blue** = MACD line, **orange** = signal line, **gray bars** = histogram
  (MACD − signal). Histogram shrinking toward zero = momentum fading even if
  price is still trending; histogram flipping sign = an actual MACD/signal
  crossover.
- `MACD_Cross_Zeroish_Up`/`Down` only fire where a crossover happens **near the
  zero line** (scaled by ATR) — in this chart, that's the crossover visible
  around late June/early July where both lines converge near zero after the
  long decline from the April/May peak, not the much larger crossover during
  the April swing from -40 to +30 (that one is too far from zero to qualify).

## 4. Trade & signal overlay (backtest result)

Generated last, in `core/pipeline.py`'s `__main__` block, by layering
`sim/viz.py::overlay_trades` + `overlay_signals_rail` on top of an `mplfinance`
candlestick chart.

<img src="/charts/trades-signals-overlay.png" alt="Trade entries/exits and signal rail over candles + volume" width={1306} height={942} style={{ width: '100%', height: 'auto' }} />

**How to read it:**
- **Triangles on the price panel** are actual *filled* trades: blue-up = long
  entry, orange-down = long exit, green-down = short entry, red-up = short
  exit. The thin connecting line between an entry and exit marker shows that
  trade's holding period and direction of P&L (rising line = profitable long,
  falling = losing long, and mirrored for shorts).
- **Bottom "Sig" rail** (labeled Long/Short) plots **every signal the compiler
  emitted**, filled or not — this is `compile_signals()`'s output, not just
  what the simulator was able to act on. Comparing the rail to the triangles
  above it shows you how many signals were emitted vs. actually filled (here,
  most emitted signals did convert to trades since `max_open_positions = 1`
  with no other position blocking).
- **Volume panel** at the bottom uses green/red bars by up/down day — the huge
  volume spike in late January lines up exactly with the price spike to ~760
  visible in chart 1, and is well above the `vol_k = 1.5×` 20-day-average
  threshold that breakout detection requires.
- Reading this chart against the per-tag table in
  [Metrics & the audit step](/docs/statistical/metrics-audit): the one clearly
  profitable trade (long entry ~540 in early May, exit ~589) is the `Hammer`
  trade that alone kept this run from a much worse total return — visually
  it's the entry/exit pair with the widest vertical rise on the chart.
